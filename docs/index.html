An open register of Formula 1 stewarding · 2025–2026

Same offence,
same penalty?

Every stewards' decision is published as a PDF with the same fields: the fact, the rule, the decision, the reason. Stewards' Room reads them, keeps each field's exact wording and where it sat in the document, runs each driver's penalty-points clock, and lines up like with like. It never says a decision was wrong. It shows you the decisions side by side.

The 2026 change, measured

For 2026 the FIA's driving guidelines kept penalty points for collisions caused by dangerous, reckless or apparently deliberate driving, and for unsportsmanlike behaviour (as reported by PlanetF1, 6 Mar 2026). So: when stewards penalised a collision under Article 2 d), how often did points come with it?

Collisions: Appendix L, Chapter IV, Article 2 d)

For each season, the outcomes given under the same article, and every decision that differs from the usual one. Where the Reason explains the difference, the explanation is quoted.

Checking the FIA's arithmetic

Each decision that gives points prints the driver's new total. The register rebuilds that total from the decisions it holds. A match confirms the history. A gap has three possible causes the register can't tell apart: it is missing earlier decisions (the usual one), a decision was changed later, or the printed figure is wrong. Gaps are shown, never patched.

    Method and limits

    How it reads a decision

    The FIA prints every decision as the same labelled table. The parser takes a label only at the table's left edge and in the table's order, and records the exact characters each field came from; the text of every fetched PDF is kept so those spans can be checked. A field that isn't printed stays empty (2025 Hungary, Doc 42 has no Fact). Wording the parser hasn't seen is flagged, never guessed. On the hand-checked set it gets – right: car, article, points, printed total and penalty. That set is laid out the way the FIA prints it; the score on real PDF layouts comes with the full fetch.

    How it watches the FIA's record

    Each fetched PDF is hashed into an append-only chain. When the FIA republishes a document, or on a full re-check, a changed file is recorded with both hashes. An edited register line is found by verification; lines cut off the end are found by comparing the chain's head with the copy published in this site's data.

    What it can't tell you

    • Whether a decision was right. Two incidents under one article can differ in ways no table captures. The register shows decisions side by side; judging them is yours.
    • Much, yet. The hand-checked set is 14 decisions, picked by hand (mostly collisions), not drawn at random. Groups under eight are marked “small sample”, and they all are.
    • The full reasoning. The register keeps short excerpts. When only an excerpt is held, a difference is marked “can't tell” rather than “unexplained”.
    • Severity in one number. Time, grid places and points aren't the same currency. Outcomes are shown as printed.
